A Local's Guide to Marrakech's Medina

June 2026

Back to Blog

Marrakech's medina can feel overwhelming on a first visit — narrow lanes, constant motion, and no clear map. Here's how to navigate it like someone who actually lives there.

Start Early, Before the Crowds

The souks are calmer and cooler before 10am. It's the best time to browse without the midday crush, and shopkeepers are often more relaxed for a chat.

Getting Lost Is Part of the Experience

The medina isn't built on a grid, and that's intentional. Rather than fighting it, treat wrong turns as a chance to stumble on a quiet courtyard or a tiny workshop you'd never find on purpose.

Where to Find the Best Mint Tea

Skip the tourist-facing cafés right on Jemaa el-Fnaa and look one or two streets back — smaller, family-run spots tend to serve tea the way locals actually drink it: strong, sweet, and poured from height.

Bargaining Basics

  • Start at roughly half the first price offered
  • Stay friendly — bargaining is a conversation, not a confrontation
  • Walking away often brings the price down further

A Few Hidden Corners

Beyond the main souks, take time for the quieter Mellah (old Jewish quarter), the tucked-away herboristes selling spices and natural remedies, and the small tanneries that rarely make it onto tourist maps.
